SAF_ARIv3.21: What Happens When Chat + Data Comes In

The stack starts with a universal event log, partitions evidence to avoid circularity, then runs trait, state, and contribution layers in parallel views.

Input

Chat + Data

Transcript, turns, timestamps, edits, model metadata, task intent, and optional telemetry become a normalized event log.

Partition

Feature Split

State-diagnostic signals feed CSPS/CSPC. Competency-diagnostic signals feed ARI. AI turns feed the CSL AI-side extractor.

Layer 1

ARI 04/08/107

107 neurons are scored as evidence, aggregated into 8 dimensions and 4 pillars for human AI-readiness and collaboration skill.

Layer 2

CSPS/CSPC States

Load, engagement, metacognition, and affect state estimate whether the observed behavior is reliable evidence or state-compromised.

Layer 3

CSL Work Layer

ARI evidence is re-projected onto ACF work levels, combined with AI displayed contribution, and scanned for emergence events.

Layer Discipline

ARI answers "how capable is the human collaborator?" CSPS/CSPC answers "what state is the person in during the session?" CSL answers "who visibly drove which cognitive work?" The retention probe answers "did this become durable human capability?" The wall matters: chat-derived process signals can be measurable today, while validated learning and sustainability require unaided follow-up data.

Urgent Questions the Framework Can Answer

The point is not to label people. The point is to protect learning, identify hidden dependence, and make collaboration auditable.

1

Is the user learning, or only producing better artifacts?

Separates output quality from durable capability through the Layer 4 retention wall.

2

Where did the human add irreplaceable value?

CSL shows foundation-backed contribution by cognitive work level, not by word count.

3

Is this efficient delegation or cognitive surrender?

CSPS/CSPC state and ARI evidence distinguish calibrated use from hollow reliance.

4

Which AI behaviors increase dependency debt?

Report patterns reveal accept-runs, low verification, and loss of metacognitive control.

5

Can a professional defend AI-assisted work?

Audit artifacts expose evidence quality, source handling, state caveats, and contribution provenance.

6

Are students using AI as a tutor or answer engine?

Prompt quality, verification behavior, and retention probes make the distinction measurable.

7

Which teams are gaining capability from AI over time?

Longitudinal ARI stability plus state trajectories reveal amplification versus stagnation.

8

Where should governance intervene first?

Risk flags identify high-stakes, low-verification, state-compromised interactions before harm scales.
